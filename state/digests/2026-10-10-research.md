## agent-safety-practice
- What matters: tiered approvals (auto-read, confirm-write, deny-destructive), sandboxing with network egress controls, treating all tool output as untrusted, and immutable audit logs of prompts, tool calls and diffs.
- Why it matters: autonomous coding agents touch secrets and repos; injection via issues, PRs and READMEs is the main breach path, and logs are the only way to reconstruct an incident.
- Verify: that no shell step runs unapproved, and that sandbox egress is deny-by-allowlist rather than open.

## actions-automation
- What matters: `schedule` cron is best-effort and can lag or be skipped on loaded runners and on inactive repos; public repos get unlimited standard-runner minutes but concurrency and usage caps still apply; default GITHUB_TOKEN permissions are now read-only.
- Why it matters: a nightly agent that silently misses cron or gets a permission denial fails without alerting; floating action tags are a live supply-chain poisoning risk.
- Verify: pin every action to a commit SHA, set explicit `permissions`, and add a cron heartbeat alert.
